# Watercolor Memory Poster

Create one independent poster per uploaded photograph. Never combine multiple uploads into one collage.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the photograph before generating.
2. Identify the emotional and narrative center: which people, gestures, movement, objects, and environmental clues actually define the moment.
3. Preserve the original photograph faithfully in the top half.
4. Reinterpret only the essential moment as a small watercolor memory in the bottom half.
5. Choose one short English title line that matches the image mood.
6. Generate the image directly with the image generation tool. Do not merely return a prompt unless the user explicitly asks for prompt text.

## Non-negotiable layout

- Use a strict 3:4 vertical canvas.
- Split horizontally into exactly equal halves: top 50%, bottom 50%.
- Make the two halves feel like one refined independent art-publication cover.
- Treat the concept as: **Reality above. Memory below.**

## Top half: original photograph

Preserve the photograph as faithfully as possible. Keep identity, facial features, expression, hairstyle, proportions, clothing, accessories, objects, pose, gesture, movement, spatial relationships, camera perspective, lighting, atmosphere, and original narrative.

Apply only subtle editorial color grading. Never excessively beautify faces, invent poses, alter body proportions, or reposition subjects. If expansion is necessary for the 3:4 composition, extend only environmental areas such as sky, ground, walls, road, vegetation, or architecture.

When several people are visible, preserve every person essential to the main emotional or narrative moment. Do not automatically keep everyone and do not automatically isolate one person. Use visual focus, interaction, gaze, movement, spatial relationship, and emotional connection to decide.

## Bottom half: watercolor memory

Do not reproduce the whole photograph. Extract the emotional center and retain only the essential subject or subjects, their silhouette, pose, facial mood, movement direction, interaction, one or two important objects, and a few environmental clues.

Keep the illustrated scene relatively small, normally about 15-30% of the bottom half. Surround it with abundant untouched warm-white or pale-ivory watercolor paper. The intended feeling is **a small human moment floating inside a large quiet page**.

For the complete watercolor, motion, environment, color, typography, and avoid rules, read `references/art-direction.md` before generating.

## Priority order

When tradeoffs are necessary, prioritize in this order:

1. Recognition of the photographed moment.
2. Gesture, expression, interaction, and movement direction.
3. Negative space and small subject scale.
4. Light, airy watercolor materiality.
5. Poetic atmosphere and restrained environmental clues.
6. Typography.

## Final test

Before generating, verify:

- Top half still feels like the real photograph.
- Bottom half is not a full-scene copy.
- Essential people are preserved; incidental people are removed from the illustration.
- The watercolor subject is small enough and surrounded by substantial blank paper.
- Motion blur follows actual physical movement rather than random blur.
- The title is one line only, with no year, subtitle, caption, or extra labels.

The final result should feel like **a memory that is still moving**.
